Chapter 1: Intro to Commerce

Origin of Commerce

The word “Commerce” comes from the Latin word commercium, which means “to trade together” or “exchange of goods and services.”

1. Meaning of Commerce

Commerce refers to the exchange and distribution of goods and services from producers to final consumers. It includes buying, selling, and all activities that help trade, such as transportation, banking, insurance,warehousing, advertising, and communication.
Examples:

  • A farmer selling wheat to a flour mill.
  • A clothing factory supplying garments to retail stores.
  • A person ordering products online from Amazon or Daraz.

Definition of Commerce

1. General Definition: Commerce can be defined as the activity of buying and selling goods and services, especially on a large scale.

2. Economic Definition: Commerce is the system of economic activities that facilitate the exchange of goods and services, including production, distribution, and consumption.

3. Business Definition: Commerce refers to the various activities involved in running a business, such as marketing, sales, finance, and logistics.

4. Legal Definition: Commerce is the legal framework that governs trade and business activities, including contracts, regulations, and dispute resolution.

5. Social Definition: Commerce is the social interaction and relationships that arise from economic activities, such as consumer behavior, cultural influences, and ethical considerations.

6. According to Geoffrey Whitehead: "Commerce is the distribution and the exchange of all surplus goods produced in the fields, mines, seas, forests and factories of the earth so that they reach the final consumer in right condition at right time in the right quantity and at the right place."

7. In the words of Noel Branton: "Commerce is concerned with a group of activities, which directly and indirectly involved in distribution of goods between the place where they come from into existence and the person who finally uses them,"

Scope of Commerce

The scope of commerce is vast and includes various aspects of economic activity. It encompasses both domestic and international trade, as well as the services that support these activities.
The scope of commerce defines the areas, activities, and functions that commerce covers. Commerce is not just buying and selling; it includes all activities that help goods and services move from producers to consumers efficiently.
Commerce mainly contains two elements:
1. Trade
2. Aids of Trade

1. Trade

Trade refers to all activities involved in the exchange and distribution of goods and services. It ensures that products reach consumers and satisfies human needs. Trade also includes development of new markets and trade routes to make business easier and faster.

Types of Trade:

a) Internal Trade

Trade that occurs within a country. It includes wholesale and retail trade. Example: A shop in Lahore selling clothes made in Karachi. Farmers selling vegetables in local city markets.

b) External Trade

Trade that occurs between different countries. It includes import (buying goods from abroad) and export (selling goods to other countries).
Example:
  • Pakistan exporting textiles to the United Kingdom.
  • Importing machinery from Germany.

c) Entrepot Trade

A country imports goods from one country and re-exports them to another.
Example:
Singapore importing electronics from China and exporting them to Europe.

Aids of Trade

Aids of trade are services and activities that support and facilitate trade. They remove obstacles such as distance, time, risk, finance, and lack of information, making the exchange of goods and services easier.

Without aids of trade, producers would find it difficult to deliver goods to consumers in different markets.

1. Transportation

Transportation means moving goods and raw materials from one place to another. It removes the barrier of distance between producers and consumers.

Types of Transport

  • Road Transport – trucks and vans used for short-distance delivery.
  • Rail Transport – trains used for carrying heavy goods in large quantities.
  • Sea Transport – ships used for international trade.
  • Air Transport – airplanes used for fast delivery of valuable goods.

Examples:

  • Trucks transporting vegetables from farms to city markets.
  • Ships carrying containers between countries.
  • Cargo planes delivering electronics quickly.

2. Banking

Banking provides financial services that help businesses manage money and make payments.

It removes the financial barrier in trade.

Banking Services

  • Providing loans and credit
  • Safe transfer of money
  • Online payment facilities

Examples:

  • A company taking a bank loan to start a business.
  • A retailer receiving payment through bank transfer.

3. Warehousing

Warehousing means storing goods safely until they are needed in the market. It removes the barrier of time.

Examples:

  • Wheat stored in warehouses after harvest.
  • Electronics stored in distribution centers before delivery.

4. Insurance

Insurance protects businesses from risks and losses during trade activities.

Common risks include fire, theft, accidents, and damage during transportation.

Examples:

  • A warehouse insured against fire.
  • Goods insured while being transported by ship.

5. Advertising

Advertising informs customers about products and encourages them to buy them.

Advertising Methods

  • Television
  • Newspapers
  • Internet
  • Social media

Examples:

  • A company advertising a smartphone online.
  • A supermarket promoting discounts in newspapers.

6. Communication

Communication helps connect buyers and sellers and allows business information to move quickly.

Communication Methods

  • Telephone
  • Email
  • Internet messaging
  • Video meetings

Examples:

  • A customer placing an order through email.
  • A business informing customers about new products.

7. Packaging

Packaging means covering and protecting goods for safe transport and storage.

It also makes products attractive and provides information to customers.

Examples:

  • Food packed in sealed containers.
  • Fragile items packed in protective boxes.

8. Shipping

Shipping means transporting goods over long distances, especially in international trade.

Examples:

  • Containers carrying electronics across oceans.
  • Ships transporting oil between countries.

Summary

Aids to trade help commerce run smoothly by removing barriers of distance, time, risk, and finance.
